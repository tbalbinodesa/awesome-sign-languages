A curated directory of high-quality sign language resources: dictionaries, learning materials, linguistic references, corpora, and software. Sign languages are full natural languages with their own grammars and communities—not universal versions of spoken languages. They must be named precisely: **Libras** is the Brazilian Sign Language, used in Brazil, and is distinct from Portuguese and from other sign languages.

This list is intentionally selective. Inclusion is based on clear provenance, usefulness, and fit for the stated purpose, not popularity or search ranking. A resource can be free to access without its data being free to download or reuse; check each project's terms.

> **Language-specific starting point:** This English version presents ASL resources first. For a Portuguese-language guide that presents Libras resources first, use the [Português (Brasil) version](README.pt-BR.md). The language of this document is not a translation direction: ASL and Libras are different languages.

## Learning and teaching

### American Sign Language (ASL)

- [ASL Connect – ASL by Topic](https://gallaudet.edu/asl-connect/topics/) - Free basic American Sign Language (ASL) vocabulary videos and self-paced lessons from Gallaudet University. Vocabulary support, not a substitute for interactive instruction.
- [Learning American Sign Language](https://clerccenter.gallaudet.edu/ndec/educational-resources/learning-asl/) - Clerc Center's guide to learning ASL, classes, and educational resources; explains the language's grammar and why in-person community practice matters.

## Dictionaries and lexical resources

### American Sign Language (ASL)

- [ASL Signbank](https://aslsignbank.com/) - Research-maintained ASL lexical resource from University of Connecticut and Gallaudet University. Public users can browse signs and translation equivalents; some data requires registration and approval. Intended for fluent signers and linguistic annotation, not a beginner course.
- [ASL-LEX](https://asl-lex.org/) - Research database documenting lexical and phonological properties of 2,723 ASL signs, with video references and searchable visualizations. Database reuse is noncommercial (CC BY-NC 4.0); reference videos are excluded from that license.

## Linguistics and reference

### American Sign Language (ASL)

- [The Linguistics of American Sign Language](https://gupress.gallaudet.edu/Books/L/Linguistics-of-American-Sign-Language2) - Gallaudet University Press textbook on ASL structure and linguistics (fifth edition). A scholarly reference; the book is not a free resource.
- [Sign Language Annotation, Archiving and Sharing (SLAASh)](https://gallaudet.edu/american-sign-language/sign-language-annotation-archiving-and-sharing-slaash/) - Gallaudet project and conventions supporting sign language video annotation, archiving, and sharing.

## Corpora and datasets

### American Sign Language (ASL)

- [YouTube-ASL](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/youtube_asl) - Google Research dataset of ASL videos with English captions, reviewed by Deaf annotators for video quality and caption alignment. The release provides video IDs, not the videos; availability, caption quality, and platform rights can vary. See the project paper and terms before research use.
- [OpenASL](https://github.com/chevalierNoir/OpenASL) - Research dataset and code for open-domain ASL-to-English translation. Access and reuse are subject to its noncommercial, no-derivatives license; check the repository for current terms and dataset limitations.

## Software and research tools

### General-purpose

- [ELAN](https://archive.mpi.nl/mpi/elan) - Professional annotation tool for audio and video, widely used in language research and compatible with sign language lexicons such as ASL Signbank. Free and open source under GPL-3.0; review its license and documentation.

## Libras resources

The resources below cover **Libras (Brazilian Sign Language)**. They are grouped here so learners, researchers, and developers do not mistake ASL materials or Portuguese-language interfaces for Libras resources.

### Learning and sharing

- [Repositório Digital do INES](http://repositorio.ines.gov.br/ilustra/) - Instituto Nacional de Educação de Surdos (INES) repository of Libras educational materials. Browse the metadata and license for each item before downloading or reusing it; formats and accessibility features vary by resource.
- [Manuário Acadêmico e Escolar](https://manuario.ines.gov.br/) - INES's subject-organized collection of Libras signs for school and academic contexts. Its signs are collected, validated with Deaf INES teachers and academic-community representatives, then recorded in studio; useful for specialized vocabulary, not a complete general dictionary.

### Dictionaries and lexical documentation

- [Dicionário da Língua Brasileira de Sinais — INES](https://dicionario.ines.gov.br/) - Searchable Libras dictionary from INES with videos, Portuguese examples and senses, subjects, grammatical classes, and sign-origin information.
- [Signbank Libras](https://signbank.levantelab.com.br/pt/about) - UFSC-linked, open-access Libras signbank connected to the Global Signbank and Libras Corpus. Supports lexical and linguistic research and can link to ELAN; it is a research lexicon rather than a prescriptive authority for every regional variant.

### Linguistics, corpora, and datasets

- [Corpus de Libras](https://corpuslibras.ufsc.br/?lang=en) - UFSC's openly accessible Libras corpus with video, photos, data, metadata, and documentation guidelines. Designed for linguistic research, professional work, and preservation; follow its access conditions and participant/data-use guidance.

### Software, access, and collaboration

- [Suíte VLibras](https://www.gov.br/governodigital/pt-br/acessibilidade-e-usuario/vlibras) - Brazilian government and UFPB/LAVID free, open-source accessibility suite that automatically renders digital Portuguese content in Libras with a 3D avatar. Includes web, desktop, and mobile tools.
- [VLibras Widget documentation](https://vlibras.gov.br/doc/widget/introduction/presentation.html) - Official integration guide for adding the Portuguese-to-Libras accessibility widget to a website.
- [WikiLibras](https://wiki.vlibras.gov.br/) - Collaborative VLibras platform for consulting signs and proposing or reviewing signs, translations, and avatar animations. Contributions enter the suite only after its expert review process.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> VLibras translates digital content **from Brazilian Portuguese to Libras**; it is not a bidirectional Portuguese–Libras interpreter and does not replace a human interpreter. It should not be used as the sole access provision in classes, legal proceedings, public-service interactions, or other high-stakes communication. Verify regional and contextual appropriateness with Libras users.

## Contributing

Contributions are welcome, especially resources for sign languages and communities underrepresented here. Please open an issue or pull request and include:

- The resource's official URL and the specific sign language(s) it covers.
- Who maintains it and why it is trustworthy (for example, a Deaf-led organization, university, established research team, or transparent community project).
- What it offers, who it is for, and whether it is free to access.
- Any restrictions on accounts, downloads, redistribution, commercial use, or data licensing.
- Evidence of quality and currency; flag known limitations such as dialect coverage, incomplete entries, or machine-generated content.

Do not submit scraped content, unverified sign translation apps, or claims that a resource translates between “sign language” and speech without naming the languages and explaining its limitations. A sign language is not universal; list each language explicitly. For automatic translation tools, state the supported direction(s), whether output is avatar-based or human-produced, and that it does not replace interpreting. Access does not imply permission to redistribute.
